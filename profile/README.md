## The shift we're built for

Quick commerce rewrote the rules of fresh produce. "Order and wait" became "order and receive in minutes." Behind every 10-minute delivery of tomatoes sits a promise that has to be kept thousands of times a day — and keeping it is not a logistics problem. It's an **operations and systems problem**.

When delivery is measured in minutes, backend reliability becomes non-negotiable. Quick-commerce and institutional buyers don't win on price. They win on **fill rate** — consistently having the right SKU, at the right grade, in the right window. Traditional agri supply, fragmented and push-based, simply can't deliver that reliability at scale.

That gap is where Mulyam operates.

---

## Co-Farming — our answer

Most of the supply chain works backwards: farmers grow, then hope to sell. We invert it.

**Co-Farming** starts with forecasted buyer demand and back-plans cultivation with farmers — shifting agriculture from a *push* model to a *pull* model. Instead of chasing the market, our farmer network grows to a plan, guided by agronomists, against demand we can already see.

The result is a supply engine that is:

- **Predictable** — volumes planned against real demand, not guesswork
- **Grade-consistent** — the right quality mix, cycle after cycle
- **Traceable** — every batch linked back to farm, crop cycle, and input history
- **Resilient and fast** — engineered for the velocity that quick commerce demands

## How it works

<div align="center">

**Buyer demand**  →  **Forecast**  →  **Co-Farming plan with farmers**  →  **Agronomist-guided cultivation**  →  **Grade-controlled harvest**  →  **QC, grading & traceability**  →  **On the shelf in minutes**

</div>

<br>

Demand signals from buyers become cultivation plans. Agronomists shepherd each crop cycle from sowing to harvest. Produce arrives at our collection centres, where it's graded, quality-checked, and consolidated — then dispatched to buyers with the reliability the modern shelf requires. Every step is captured as data, and that data compounds into an ever-sharper picture of what to grow, where, and when.

## Traceability, by design

Traceability isn't a compliance checkbox for us — it's a competitive moat and, increasingly, a public-health contribution. Our architecture captures the full chain — farm, crop cycle, activities, harvest lot — so a batch of produce can be traced back to exactly where and how it was grown.

That foundation is what makes **residue-conscious sourcing** credible rather than a claim: cultivation follows agronomist-designed recipes, inputs are logged, and produce carries its provenance with it. Trust, transparency, and premium positioning fall out of the same system.
